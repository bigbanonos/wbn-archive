Women's Baseball World Cup
WBSC Women's Baseball World Cup 2026 · Two Groups · Two Continents

The pinnacle of women's international baseball. Held every two years, the WBSC Women's Baseball World Cup brings together the top women's national teams in a true world championship. The 2026 edition splits into two qualifying groups — Group A in Rockford, Illinois (July 22–27) and Group B in Tainan, Taiwan (August 23–27) — with a Super Round and Final Stage to crown the champion. This pairs perfectly with the inaugural WPBL season launching this August in Springfield, IL, marking 2026 as the most consequential year for women's baseball in modern history.

Tournament Format

Group A — Rockford, Illinois 🇺🇸
July 22–27, 2026
Rockford, Illinois — the same city that produced the legendary Rockford Peaches of the AAGPBL (1943-1954) and inspired A League of Their Own. The historical resonance is intentional: WBSC chose Rockford as Group A host to honor the deepest roots of American women's professional baseball. Group A teams compete in round-robin pool play to advance to the Super Round.
Group B — Tainan, Taiwan 🇹🇼
August 23–27, 2026
Tainan, Taiwan — host to one of the most baseball-passionate cities in Asia. Group B brings together Pacific and Asian women's baseball nations including the defending champion Japan, and host nation Chinese Taipei. Group B teams advance from pool play to the Super Round.
Super Round + Finals
September 2026 (TBD)
The top finishers from each group advance to a Super Round, with the bracket culminating in a championship final. Final location and exact dates to be confirmed by WBSC. Japan has won 6 of the last 7 Women's Baseball World Cups — the United States, Canada, and Chinese Taipei are the perennial challengers.
The Fact
2026 Is the Single Most Consequential Year for Women's Baseball Since 1954
Two milestone events are happening within weeks of each other in the United States: the WBSC Women's Baseball World Cup Group A in Rockford, Illinois (July 22-27) — a city chosen specifically to honor the legacy of the AAGPBL Rockford Peaches — and the inaugural Women's Pro Baseball League (WPBL) season launching August 2026 in Springfield, Illinois. That's two cities in central Illinois, three weeks apart, marking the first time since the 1954 collapse of the All-American Girls Professional Baseball League that women's professional baseball has had this kind of institutional momentum in the United States. Japan has dominated the international game for two decades, winning six of the last seven Women's Baseball World Cup titles — but the convergence of WPBL and World Cup hosting in 2026 signals that the American women's baseball pipeline is finally being rebuilt in a serious way for the first time in 72 years.
